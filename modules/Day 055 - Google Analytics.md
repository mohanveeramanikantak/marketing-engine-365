# Day 055 - Google Analytics

## Introduction

Google Analytics (GA) is one of the most widely used web analytics platforms developed by Google. It helps businesses understand how visitors interact with websites and applications.

Google Analytics collects data about users, sessions, traffic sources, user behavior, events, conversions, and customer journeys. Marketers use this information to make data-driven decisions and improve marketing performance.

In 2023, Google officially replaced Universal Analytics (UA) with **Google Analytics 4 (GA4)**. GA4 uses an event-based data model, making it more flexible and suitable for modern websites and mobile applications.

In college marketing, Google Analytics can help track:

* Admission campaigns
* Landing page performance
* Webinar registrations
* Scholarship campaigns
* Social media traffic
* Student engagement

---

# What is Google Analytics?

Google Analytics can be defined as:

> "A web analytics platform developed by Google that collects, measures, analyzes, and reports website and application data."

It helps businesses answer:

* Who visits the website?
* Where do visitors come from?
* Which pages are most popular?
* How long do users stay?
* Which campaigns generate leads?
* Where do users drop off?

---

# Why Google Analytics is Important

Google Analytics is important because it helps marketers make decisions using real data.

## Benefits

* Understand customer behavior

* Improve website performance
* Increase ROI

Without analytics, businesses rely on assumptions.

# Universal Analytics vs GA4

| Universal Analytics       | GA4                         |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Session-based model       | Event-based model           |
| Separate mobile analytics | Unified web + app analytics |
| Limited AI features       | AI-powered insights         |
| Bounce Rate focused       | Engagement focused          |
| Less predictive           | Predictive analytics        |

GA4 is more flexible and future-ready.

---

# Event-Based Data Model

GA4 records everything as an event.

Examples:

* page_view
* scroll
* click
* video_start
* form_submit
* purchase
* login

This provides more detailed tracking than traditional session-based models.

# Important User Metrics

GA4 tracks several user metrics.

---

## Users

Unique visitors to the website.

Example:

10,000 people visit the website.

Users = 10,000

---

## New Users

Visitors coming for the first time.

Useful for:

* Measuring awareness campaigns
* Tracking new audience growth

---

## Active Users

Users who actively engage with the website.

GA4 focuses heavily on Active Users.

---

## Sessions

A session is a group of user interactions.

Example:

One person visits three times.

Users = 1

Sessions = 3

---

## Engaged Sessions

Sessions lasting:

* More than 10 seconds
  OR
* Two or more page views
  OR
* One conversion

GA4 prioritizes engagement over bounce rate.

---

## Average Engagement Time

Measures:

> How long users actively engage with content.

Higher engagement often indicates:

* Better content
* Good UX
* Higher interest

# Engagement Reports

Engagement reports show user interactions.

Important metrics:

---

## Page Views

Number of pages viewed.

Example:

User opens:

* Homepage
* Courses Page
* Admissions Page

Page Views = 3

---

## Events

Events track actions.

Examples:

* Scroll
* Video play
* Button click
* Form submission
* Download brochure

---

## Scroll Tracking

Tracks how far users scroll.

Example:

Users scroll:

* 25%
* 50%
* 75%
* 90%

Useful for:

* Content optimization
* Landing page improvements

---

## Bounce Rate

Bounce Rate measures:

> Percentage of users who leave without meaningful engagement.

Formula:

\text{Bounce Rate} = \frac{\text{Non-engaged Sessions}}{\text{Total Sessions}} \times 100

Lower bounce rate is generally better.

---

## Engagement Rate

Engagement Rate measures:

> Percentage of engaged sessions.

Formula:

\text{Engagement Rate} = \frac{\text{Engaged Sessions}}{\text{Total Sessions}} \times 100

Higher engagement indicates better user experience.

---

# Funnel Exploration

Funnels show how users move through stages.

Example:

```text
Website Visit
      ↓
Course Page Visit
      ↓
Admission Page
      ↓
Inquiry Form Open
      ↓
Inquiry Submitted
```

Funnels help identify drop-off points.

---

# Path Exploration

Path Exploration answers:

> What pages do users visit before and after a specific page?

Example:

Homepage

↓

Courses Page

↓

Admissions Page

↓

Inquiry Form

This helps understand user journeys.
